---
title: "Botchan (Master Darling)"
author: "Soseki Natsume"
year: 1906
book_slug: "botchan-master-darling"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Botchan"
    role: "Narrator and protagonist; a new schoolmaster"
    summary: "A hot-tempered Tokyo man who grew up reckless and unfavoured by his family and takes a post as a mathematics teacher at a middle school in Shikoku. He nicknames his colleagues, is teased by students, and moves from boarding house to boarding house. He sides with Porcupine against Red Shirt, resigns after a brawl and returns to Tokyo."
    analysis: "Botchan is an honest, impulsive man whose straightforward sense of justice clashes with the petty politics of the school. His blunt narration is funny, and it also points to the novel's critique of hypocrisy in Meiji society. His failures show the cost of principle without tact."
  - name: "Kiyo"
    role: "The family's old maid; Botchan's devoted supporter"
    summary: "An elderly servant who loves Botchan despite his bad reputation and tells him he will be a great man. She gives him sweets and money, writes to him after he leaves, and welcomes him back to Tokyo when he resigns. She dies of pneumonia soon after, and he has her buried at the family temple."
    analysis: "Kiyo is the book's emotional anchor, the only person who sees Botchan's worth. Her unquestioning love contrasts with the cynicism of the school and explains why he remains a decent man. His thoughts of her are his moral compass."
  - name: "Porcupine"
    role: "The head of mathematics; Botchan's colleague and eventual ally"
    summary: "A stocky, bristling teacher from Aizu whom Botchan first dislikes after a quarrel about the boarding house. He proves honest and courageous, defending Botchan in the staff meeting and apologising for believing false claims. He resigns with him and helps him take revenge on Red Shirt."
    analysis: "Porcupine represents the straight-talking samurai spirit that Botchan respects, and his friendship shows that integrity can be found even in a place full of intrigue. His loyalty, and his honest disagreement with Botchan, make him the book's most trustworthy character."
  - name: "Red Shirt"
    role: "The head teacher; a scheming rival"
    summary: "The school's smooth, educated head teacher who wears a red flannel shirt and speaks of refined pleasures. He engineers Koga's transfer to Nobeoka so that he can court the Madonna, offers Botchan a bribe in the form of a raise, and uses a newspaper to smear Botchan and Porcupine. He is exposed and beaten on a back road."
    analysis: "Red Shirt is the embodiment of hypocrisy and the abuse of status, and his culture is a mask for self-interest. Botchan's revenge on him is the novel's vindication of plain decency over polished corruption."
  - name: "Clown"
    role: "A teacher; Red Shirt's flatterer"
    summary: "A fawning teacher of drawing who follows Red Shirt and echoes his opinions. He joins the fishing trip, carries tales, and is beaten along with Red Shirt in the climactic scene."
    analysis: "Clown is a comic toady whose servility completes the picture of Red Shirt's faction. He shows how corruption needs willing followers."
  - name: "Badger"
    role: "The principal"
    summary: "The school's head, who lectures Botchan on the spirit of education and relies on Red Shirt's advice. He is cautious and does little to punish the students or his favourite, hoping to avoid trouble."
    analysis: "Badger stands for weak leadership: pompous, passive and easily manipulated. His inaction allows Red Shirt's intrigues to flourish."
  - name: "Koga (Hubbard Squash)"
    role: "A teacher of English; the victim of Red Shirt's scheme"
    summary: "A gentle, pale teacher whose engagement to the Madonna is broken when Red Shirt takes an interest. He is transferred against his will to Nobeoka, and Botchan attends his farewell dinner."
    analysis: "Koga is the innocent whose misfortune exposes Red Shirt's manipulation. His quiet acceptance contrasts with Botchan's fury and gives the plot its moral urgency."
  - name: "The Madonna"
    role: "Koga's fiancée; the object of Red Shirt's pursuit"
    summary: "A beautiful woman engaged to Koga until Red Shirt, who is better placed in society, begins to court her. Botchan sees her at the hot springs with Red Shirt and realises what has happened."
    analysis: "She is largely seen through the men's talk, as a prize in their competition. Her situation shows how social position and gossip shape marriage in the town."
