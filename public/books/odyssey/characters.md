---
title: "The Odyssey"
author: "Homer"
year: -800
book_slug: "odyssey"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Ulysses (Odysseus)"
    role: "Hero; king of Ithaca"
    summary: "The cunning king who fought at Troy and takes ten more years to get home. He blinds Polyphemus, escapes Circe, visits the dead, passes the Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, and loses every ship and companion before reaching Ithaca, where he returns disguised as a beggar. He tests his household, strings the bow, kills the suitors with Telemachus and reclaims his wife and kingdom."
    analysis: "Ulysses embodies metis, practical intelligence, over brute strength: he survives by disguise, deceit and endurance. His journey also tests his loyalty to home against every temptation to forget it, including Calypso's offer of immortality, and the poem can be read as defining a hero by what he is willing to go back to."
  - name: "Penelope"
    role: "Ulysses' wife; queen of Ithaca"
    summary: "She waits twenty years for Ulysses while more than a hundred suitors consume his estate. She delays remarriage by unweaving the shroud for Laertes by night, bargains gifts out of the suitors, tests the disguised stranger with questions and dreams, and finally proposes the bow contest. She accepts Ulysses only after he describes the immovable bed."
    analysis: "Penelope matches her husband in intelligence and guile, so the reunion is a meeting of equals rather than a reward. Her fidelity is active resistance, and the bed test shows that she is as hard to deceive as he is, giving the poem's ending its emotional weight."
  - name: "Telemachus"
    role: "Ulysses' son"
    summary: "A young man who begins as a passive victim of the suitors until Minerva urges him to call an assembly and go to Pylos and Sparta for news of his father. He escapes the suitors' ambush on his return, meets Ulysses at Eumaeus's hut, and fights beside him in the final battle."
    analysis: "The poem's early books chart his passage into manhood, so his journey is a mirror of the one his father makes. He is also a test of inheritance: by the time of the slaughter he has earned his place beside Ulysses, which secures the future of the house."
  - name: "Minerva (Athena)"
    role: "Goddess of wisdom; Ulysses' divine patron"
    summary: "She appears as Mentes and as Mentor to guide Telemachus, argues for Ulysses' release from Calypso in the divine council, disguises Ulysses on Ithaca and stands with him during the slaughter. She finally halts the fighting with the suitors' kin and imposes peace."
    analysis: "Minerva's favour shows that cleverness is rewarded by the gods, and she treats Ulysses as a kindred spirit. Her interventions make human struggle part of a larger moral order in which justice for the suitors' abuses is divinely sanctioned."
  - name: "Neptune (Poseidon)"
    role: "God of the sea; Ulysses' divine enemy"
    summary: "Angered by the blinding of his son Polyphemus, he wrecks Ulysses' raft after Calypso releases him and prolongs the voyage with storms. He later punishes the Phaeacians for helping Ulysses by turning their returning ship to stone."
    analysis: "Neptune supplies the divine opposition that makes the homecoming a trial, balancing Minerva's support. His anger shows that actions have consequences, and that Ulysses' pride at the cave of Polyphemus has a price."
  - name: "Antinous"
    role: "Leader of the suitors"
    summary: "The most arrogant of the suitors, he plots to ambush Telemachus, blames Penelope for the weaving trick, and strikes the disguised beggar with a footstool. He is the first to die when Ulysses shoots him through the throat as he lifts a cup."
    analysis: "Antinous is the clearest violation of the rules of hospitality that govern the poem. His death at the moment he is feasting marks the point where justice reverses the situation, and his sins stand for the whole group."
  - name: "Eumaeus"
    role: "The loyal swineherd"
    summary: "A servant, once a king's son stolen by Phoenician traders, who keeps Ulysses' pigs and welcomes the disguised stranger in his hut. He does not believe the beggar's stories about Ulysses, yet treats him as a guest sent by Jove, and later fights alongside him against the suitors."
    analysis: "Eumaeus is the model of faithful service and proper hospitality, in contrast with the suitors and disloyal servants. His loyalty is based on affection rather than status, and his reward after the battle shows the poem's moral economy."
  - name: "Euryclea (Eurycleia)"
    role: "Ulysses' old nurse"
    summary: "She nursed both Ulysses and Telemachus and helps Telemachus prepare for his voyage. While washing the stranger's feet she recognises the scar from his boyhood boar hunt, and after the slaughter she identifies the disloyal maids and tells Penelope that Ulysses has returned."
    analysis: "Euryclea's recognition, through the scar, links Ulysses' disguise to his true history and body. She also dramatises the household's divided loyalties, and her joy and her judgement of the maids show the cost of those divisions."
  - name: "Polyphemus"
    role: "The Cyclops; son of Neptune"
    summary: "A one-eyed giant who traps Ulysses and his men in his cave and eats several of them. Ulysses tells him his name is 'Noman,' blinds him with a heated stake, and escapes under the sheep. Polyphemus prays to his father Neptune to keep Ulysses from home."
    analysis: "Polyphemus is a monstrous opposite of civilisation, with no respect for gods or guests, and Ulysses defeats him by wit. His curse also moves the plot, and Ulysses' boast of his real name as he leaves turns a triumph into the cause of his later suffering."
  - name: "Circe"
    role: "Sorceress of Aeaea"
    summary: "She turns Ulysses' men into pigs, but Ulysses, armed with the herb moly from Mercury, forces her to restore them. She hosts the crew for a year, then sends Ulysses to consult Teiresias in the underworld and warns him about the Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis."
    analysis: "Circe tests whether Ulysses can remain himself when offered pleasure and comfort. She turns from threat to guide, so the poem shows hospitality and wisdom as a power that must be won."
  - name: "Calypso"
    role: "Nymph of Ogygia"
    summary: "She keeps Ulysses on her island for years and offers him immortality if he stays as her husband. When Jove orders his release through Mercury, she reluctantly helps him build a raft."
    analysis: "Calypso offers the opposite of homecoming, a changeless existence without death or memory. Ulysses' refusal shows that he values his mortal, human life and his marriage above a god's gift."
  - name: "Nausicaa"
    role: "Princess of the Phaeacians"
    summary: "Inspired by Minerva to wash clothes at the river, she finds the shipwrecked Ulysses, gives him clothing and food, and tells him how to approach her parents in the palace. She says farewell to him as he prepares to leave Scheria."
    analysis: "Nausicaa is the poem's picture of grace and good judgement in a young woman, and a possible marriage that Ulysses cannot take. Her kindness begins his rescue and shows the hospitality that Ithaca's suitors lack."
  - name: "Alcinous"
    role: "King of the Phaeacians"
    summary: "He receives Ulysses at his palace, hosts games and a banquet, and listens as Ulysses tells the tale of his wanderings. He arranges a Phaeacian ship to carry him home, loaded with gifts."
    analysis: "Alcinous is a model host whose court gives Ulysses an audience for his story. The Phaeacians' hospitality stands in contrast with the suitors, and their later punishment by Neptune shows the cost of helping a man the god hates."
  - name: "Teiresias"
    role: "Blind prophet of Thebes"
    summary: "Ulysses summons his ghost in the underworld, where Teiresias warns him that Neptune's anger and the cattle of the sun god will bring trouble, and that he will reach Ithaca alone and find trouble in his house."
    analysis: "Teiresias gives the middle of the poem its sense of fate, setting the terms for Ulysses' remaining trials. His prophecy shows that knowledge, like the hero's cunning, has to be paid for in suffering."
