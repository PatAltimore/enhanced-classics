---
title: "Hamlet"
author: "William Shakespeare"
year: 1603
book_slug: "hamlet"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Hamlet"
    role: "Prince of Denmark; the avenger"
    summary: "The son of the dead King Hamlet, who returns from university to find his father dead and his mother married to his uncle. The Ghost tells him Claudius murdered his father, and Hamlet vows revenge. He feigns madness, stages The Mousetrap to test Claudius, accidentally kills Polonius, and is sent to England. He returns, duels Laertes and dies, having killed Claudius."
    analysis: "Hamlet's long delay turns a revenge plot into a study of doubt, grief and the limits of action. His soliloquies make thought itself the drama, while his cruelty to Ophelia and Gertrude shows how corrosive his anger becomes. His acceptance of fate near the end, 'the readiness is all', marks his change."
  - name: "Claudius"
    role: "King of Denmark; Hamlet's uncle and stepfather"
    summary: "Hamlet's father's brother, who murdered him with poison in the ear, took the throne and married Queen Gertrude. A capable ruler, he spies on Hamlet through Rosencrantz and Guildenstern, sends him to England to be killed, and then plots with Laertes to poison him. He dies when Hamlet stabs him and forces him to drink the poison."
    analysis: "Claudius is a politician, not a monster, and his attempt to pray shows real remorse he cannot act on. He is Hamlet's foil: decisive and calculating where Hamlet hesitates. He stands for the 'rottenness' of Denmark, a state poisoned at its head."
  - name: "Gertrude"
    role: "Queen of Denmark; Hamlet's mother"
    summary: "The widow of King Hamlet who married his brother within two months, to her son's disgust. She is affectionate and anxious about Hamlet, tries to understand his behaviour, and is shaken when he confronts her in her closet. She describes Ophelia's drowning and dies accidentally after drinking the poisoned cup meant for Hamlet."
    analysis: "Gertrude's guilt, or innocence, is left unclear, which makes her marriage the centre of Hamlet's disgust at women and sexuality. She is loyal to both husband and son, and her death shows how the king's plots destroy those around him."
  - name: "Ophelia"
    role: "Polonius's daughter; Hamlet's beloved"
    summary: "A young woman who is told by her father and brother to distrust Hamlet's affection. She obeys them, returns his letters, and is attacked verbally by Hamlet in the nunnery scene. After her father's death by Hamlet's hand she goes mad, sings fragments of ballads, and drowns in a brook."
    analysis: "Ophelia is caught between the men who control her, and her breakdown shows the human cost of their intrigues. Her songs and flowers say what she cannot say openly. Her death spurs Laertes and provokes Hamlet's declaration of love at her grave."
  - name: "Polonius"
    role: "Lord Chamberlain; father of Ophelia and Laertes"
    summary: "The king's chief councillor, who gives his children advice, sends Reynaldo to spy on Laertes, and theorises that Hamlet is mad for love of Ophelia. He arranges for the King and himself to overhear Hamlet with her. Hiding behind an arras in Gertrude's room, he is stabbed by Hamlet, who thinks he is the King."
    analysis: "Polonius is a comic figure of windy wisdom, and also a dangerous one, since his spying leads to disaster. His death sets Laertes against Hamlet and unhinges Ophelia, so a bumbling man's end causes the tragedy's final run of deaths."
  - name: "Laertes"
    role: "Polonius's son; Ophelia's brother"
    summary: "A young nobleman who returns from France to avenge his father's death. He storms the castle, grieves over Ophelia's madness and is easily recruited by Claudius to kill Hamlet with a poisoned, unbated sword. He wounds Hamlet and is wounded in turn, and forgives him before he dies."
    analysis: "Laertes is Hamlet's foil: another son avenging a father, but acting at once and without scruple. His willingness to use a poisoned blade shows how easily revenge corrupts. His death and forgiveness give the final scene a sense of reconciliation."
  - name: "Horatio"
    role: "Hamlet's loyal friend"
    summary: "A scholar and friend from Wittenberg who first sees the Ghost with the sentinels and tells Hamlet. He is trusted to watch Claudius during The Mousetrap and stays by Hamlet to the end. He tries to drink the poisoned cup after Hamlet dies, but is asked to live and tell the story."
    analysis: "Horatio is the stable, balanced man Hamlet admires, who is not 'passion's slave'. He acts as witness and later as narrator, so that the tragedy will be remembered correctly. His survival gives the play its final voice."
  - name: "The Ghost of King Hamlet"
    role: "Hamlet's dead father"
    summary: "The spirit of the former king who appears to the sentinels and to Hamlet on the battlements. He says he was murdered in his orchard by his brother and demands revenge, while asking that Gertrude be spared. He appears again in Gertrude's closet to remind Hamlet of his purpose."
    analysis: "The Ghost sets the plot in motion and raises the question of whether it can be trusted, which is why Hamlet needs a test. He represents the demands of the past and the duty of vengeance that Hamlet finds so hard."
  - name: "Rosencrantz and Guildenstern"
    role: "Hamlet's school friends turned royal spies"
    summary: "Two courtiers summoned by Claudius to find out what troubles Hamlet. They agree to accompany him to England carrying a letter ordering his death. Hamlet finds the letter and rewrites it, so they are executed instead."
    analysis: "They show how friendship is corrupted by power, and Hamlet's cold response measures how far his experience has hardened him. They are interchangeable, which makes them a comedy of eager servility and also a symbol of the court's dishonesty."
  - name: "Fortinbras"
    role: "Prince of Norway"
    summary: "The young Norwegian prince whose father was killed by King Hamlet. He is kept from attacking Denmark by his uncle and marches through Denmark to fight in Poland, a sight that shames Hamlet. At the end he arrives to find the royal family dead and claims the throne."
    analysis: "Fortinbras is the man of action opposed to Hamlet's thought, and his ambition for 'a little patch of ground' shows the cost of political force. He brings the tragedy to an orderly end, with a new ruler to replace the dead."
  - name: "The Gravedigger"
    role: "Clown and sexton in the churchyard"
    summary: "A witty labourer digging Ophelia's grave who jokes with Hamlet and Horatio about death. He unearths the skull of Yorick, the king's jester, which prompts Hamlet's meditation on mortality. He dates his work to the day Hamlet was born."
    analysis: "The Gravedigger brings humour and plain wisdom to the tragedy, and his indifference to rank shows death's equality. Yorick's skull gives Hamlet an image of the end he has been contemplating."
