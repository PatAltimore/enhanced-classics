---
title: "The Call of the Wild"
author: "Jack London"
year: 1903
book_slug: "the-call-of-the-wild"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Buck"
    role: "Protagonist; a St. Bernard-Scotch shepherd cross"
    summary: "A powerful dog who lives comfortably on Judge Miller's estate in California until he is stolen and sold into the Klondike trade. He learns the law of club and fang, becomes a sled dog, defeats Spitz for the lead, and survives a brutal run of owners. After John Thornton's death, he joins a wolf pack and becomes a legend."
    analysis: "Buck's story is a parable of regression: he sheds civilisation and rediscovers ancestral instincts. London presents the change as both loss and fulfilment, with Buck gaining freedom as he loses his bond with humans. He also embodies the book's belief in survival through adaptation."
  - name: "John Thornton"
    role: "Buck's last and beloved master"
    summary: "A gold prospector who rescues Buck from the abusive Hal, cuts him free before the sled breaks through the ice, and nurses him back to health. Buck saves his life and wins him a bet by pulling a thousand-pound sled. Thornton leads a search for the Lost Cabin and is killed by the Yeehats."
    analysis: "Thornton is the only man Buck loves, which makes him the last tie to civilisation. His death frees Buck to answer the wild, and the contrast between his loyal kindness and the other humans' carelessness shows what Buck gives up."
  - name: "Spitz"
    role: "The lead dog; Buck's rival"
    summary: "A white husky from Spitzbergen who leads the sled team and bullies the others. He is cunning and cruel, and watches Buck as a threat. After a long feud and the death of Curly, Buck challenges him in a fight to the death and takes his place."
    analysis: "Spitz shows what the Northland demands: dominance achieved by strength and cunning. His defeat marks Buck's emergence as a leader, and the fight is a turning point in his transformation."
  - name: "François and Perrault"
    role: "French-Canadian mail couriers; Buck's first northern masters"
    summary: "Two fair-minded couriers who buy Buck and train him to work in the sled team. François teaches him the commands, and Perrault values his strength. They help Buck rise to lead dog and set record-breaking runs before they are reassigned and Buck is sold on."
    analysis: "They represent the best kind of trail-hardened human, firm but just. Their disappearance teaches Buck that his masters are transient, which fuels his fear of abandonment and increases his later devotion to Thornton."
  - name: "Hal, Charles and Mercedes"
    role: "Inexperienced gold-seekers; Buck's cruel owners"
    summary: "A trio from the States who buy the team in Skaguay and overload their sled with unnecessary goods. They mismanage the dogs, starve them and quarrel among themselves. Thornton warns them not to cross the rotten ice, but they push on and fall through with the sled and the dogs."
    analysis: "They are the book's picture of ignorance and folly, a contrast to the competence of Thornton and the couriers. Their deaths show that the wild punishes the unprepared, while Buck's rescue shows that loyalty can be a form of wisdom."
  - name: "Dave"
    role: "A sled dog; Buck's teammate"
    summary: "A steady, sullen wheel dog who works the sled with silent pride. When he falls ill and is unable to pull, he insists on staying in harness, and the driver, respecting his wish, lets him run until he collapses. He is shot and the team moves on."
    analysis: "Dave's death shows the dignity of work and loyalty among the dogs and the ruthlessness of the trail. His refusal to give up is both admirable and sad, and it affects Buck's sense of what a dog owes his task."
  - name: "The Man in the Red Sweater"
    role: "Buck's first trainer"
    summary: "A man who breaks Buck's spirit with a club in a crate in the Seattle port. He beats Buck into submission, showing him that a man with a club must be obeyed, but not necessarily loved."
    analysis: "His club is Buck's first lesson in the law of force, and it prepares him for the harsh code of the North. The scene marks the end of Buck's sheltered life and the start of his education."
  - name: "The Yeehats"
    role: "A native tribe in the northern wilderness"
    summary: "A group of Indians who attack Thornton's camp, kill him and his partners, and are in turn attacked by Buck, who kills several. Afterwards they come to fear a 'Ghost Dog' that haunts their valley."
    analysis: "They are the last threat that Buck faces, and in avenging Thornton he breaks his final human bond. Their legend of the Ghost Dog shows his transformation into a creature of myth."
  - name: "Judge Miller and Manuel"
    role: "Buck's first owner and the man who betrays him"
    summary: "Buck lives happily on Judge Miller's estate in the Santa Clara Valley as king of the place. Manuel, a gardener's helper with a gambling habit, sells him to a stranger for money, and the fall into captivity begins."
    analysis: "They mark the world Buck loses, a world of comfort and order, and the betrayal that sends him north. Manuel's selfish act shows that Buck's civilised world rested on trust that could be bought."
