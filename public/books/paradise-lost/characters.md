---
title: "Paradise Lost"
author: "John Milton"
year: 1667
book_slug: "paradise-lost"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Satan"
    role: "Rebel archangel; the tempter"
    summary: "Once the brightest of the angels, he led a rebellion against God and was cast into Hell. He rallies his followers, undertakes to corrupt the newly created humans, and crosses Chaos to Eden. Disguised as a serpent, he persuades Eve to eat the forbidden fruit, and returns to Hell in triumph only to be turned into a serpent."
    analysis: "Satan is the poem's most vivid character, with heroic speeches and real suffering, so that readers feel his appeal and then see it unravel into self-deception. His slide from rebel leader to crawling serpent shows how pride destroys the self."
  - name: "God the Father"
    role: "The Creator; the sovereign of Heaven"
    summary: "God foresees the Fall and explains to the Son that he has made humans free, so that they are responsible for their choices. He sends Raphael to warn Adam, then decrees that Adam and Eve must leave Paradise and sends Michael to guide them."
    analysis: "Milton's God defends the principle of free will and justice, and his long speeches have been criticised as cold. They are also the poem's argument that the Fall was a choice, and that grace follows from freely given mercy."
  - name: "The Son"
    role: "God's Son; the Word and future Saviour"
    summary: "The Son volunteers to die for humankind's sin, offers himself as ransom, and wins the war in Heaven by driving out the rebels. He creates the world, judges Adam and Eve, and clothes them in skins."
    analysis: "The Son embodies mercy and sacrifice, and his offer balances the justice of God's decree. His role in the war and the creation shows his authority and shapes the poem's account of redemption."
  - name: "Adam"
    role: "The first man"
    summary: "A rational, affectionate man who tends the garden, studies with the angel Raphael and loves Eve. When he learns that Eve has eaten the fruit, he deliberately joins her, fearing to lose her. After the Fall he blames her, then repents and leaves Paradise with her."
    analysis: "Adam's fall comes not from deception but from love placed above obedience, which makes it more human and more tragic. His growth from blame to forgiveness is central to the poem's final hope."
  - name: "Eve"
    role: "The first woman; Adam's wife"
    summary: "Created from Adam's rib, she is the first to be tempted. She asks to work alone in the garden, is flattered by the serpent, and eats the fruit, which she then shares with Adam. After the Fall she proposes forgiveness and begins their repentance."
    analysis: "Eve is the object of Milton's most debated treatment: she is intelligent, curious and loving, and her fall can be read as the result of Adam's weakness as well as her own. Her gesture of self-offering at the end begins the healing."
  - name: "Raphael"
    role: "Archangel; the friendly teacher"
    summary: "The 'sociable spirit' sent to warn Adam about Satan. He eats with Adam and Eve, tells the story of the war in Heaven and the creation of the world, and answers Adam's questions about the stars."
    analysis: "Raphael serves as both storyteller and moral guide, giving Milton a way to present the poem's backstory. His gentle but firm warnings about knowledge and love show what Adam knows and fails to heed."
  - name: "Michael"
    role: "Archangel; the leader of Heaven's armies"
    summary: "Michael defeats Satan's forces in the war in Heaven and is later sent to drive Adam and Eve from Eden. He shows Adam a vision of the future and teaches him that God is present everywhere."
    analysis: "Michael represents divine justice delivered with compassion, and his role in the exile prepares the poem's final lesson: Paradise can be lost, but hope remains."
  - name: "Abdiel"
    role: "Loyal angel; the lone dissenter"
    summary: "A seraph who argues against Satan's rebellion, then flies back to Heaven alone. God praises his faithfulness, and in the battle he is the first to strike Satan."
    analysis: "Abdiel is the model of steadfast loyalty, a counterexample to Satan and to the pull of numbers. His courage shows that obedience can be a moral choice and not just submission."
  - name: "Sin and Death"
    role: "Satan's daughter and their son; guardians of Hell's gate"
    summary: "Sin, born from Satan's head and a monstrous woman above and serpent below, guards Hell's gate with her son Death. They let Satan pass, then build a bridge across Chaos to Earth to follow him."
    analysis: "They are allegories made into characters, showing how pride begets sin and sin begets death. Their grotesque family is Milton's parody of the Trinity."
  - name: "Beelzebub, Moloch, Belial and Mammon"
    role: "Fallen angels; debaters in Pandaemonium"
    summary: "Beelzebub is Satan's second-in-command, who proposes to corrupt humankind. In the council, Moloch urges open war, Belial argues for ease and patience, and Mammon proposes building a kingdom in Hell."
    analysis: "Each speaker represents a temptation or a failing: violence, sloth and greed. Their debate shows the politics of a Hell where Satan always steers the outcome."
