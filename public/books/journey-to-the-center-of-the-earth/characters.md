---
title: "Journey to the Center of the Earth"
author: "Jules Verne"
year: 1864
book_slug: "journey-to-the-center-of-the-earth"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Professor Hardwigg"
    role: "Eccentric scientist; the expedition's leader (Lidenbrock in other versions)"
    summary: "Harry's uncle, a passionate mineralogist who loves his specimens and flies into rages. He finds a Runic manuscript in an Icelandic book, sets about decoding it, and learns that the alchemist Arne Saknussemm reached the Earth's interior through the volcano Sneffels. He organises the expedition, leads the descent, and shares his last drops of water with Harry."
    analysis: "The Professor embodies the nineteenth-century faith in science and the single-minded obsession of the explorer. His mix of tyranny and tenderness makes him both comic and sympathetic, and his confidence drives the plot against every practical objection."
  - name: "Harry (Axel)"
    role: "Narrator; the Professor's nephew and assistant"
    summary: "A young scholar who lives in his uncle's house and is engaged to Gretchen. He accidentally solves the cryptogram by reading it backwards and then reluctantly agrees to go. He suffers vertigo, fear and thirst underground, but gains courage from his companions and his uncle's example."
    analysis: "Harry is the sceptical, human voice who sees the dangers his uncle ignores, and his fears keep the adventure vivid for the reader. His growth from timid nephew to participant gives the story its coming-of-age thread."
  - name: "Hans Bjelke"
    role: "Icelandic eider-down hunter; the expedition's guide"
    summary: "A calm, taciturn Icelander who is hired to guide the party across the island and down into the volcano. He leads steadily, handles ropes and equipment with ease, and finds the underground stream when the water runs out. The party names it the Hansbach in his honour."
    analysis: "Hans is the model of quiet competence, and his stoicism contrasts with the Professor's enthusiasm and Harry's anxiety. His practical judgement saves the expedition more than once, and he stands for the strength of ordinary skill."
  - name: "Arne Saknussemm"
    role: "Sixteenth-century Icelandic alchemist; the original explorer"
    summary: "An Icelandic scholar whose coded note, found in a manuscript, describes his own journey to the center of the Earth through Sneffels. He is later revealed to have been persecuted for heresy, and his writings were burned in Copenhagen. The travellers find his name carved in stone."
    analysis: "Saknussemm provides the map, the challenge and the sense of a hidden past. His coded message sets the quest in motion, and finding his mark underground confirms the Professor's faith and gives the adventure its historical depth."
  - name: "Gretchen"
    role: "The Professor's goddaughter; Harry's fiancée"
    summary: "A gentle young woman who lives in the Professor's house and is loved by Harry. She encourages him to go, believing the journey is a duty, and waits for them at home."
    analysis: "Gretchen gives Harry a reason to be brave and to return, and her faith in the expedition shapes his decision. She is also a quiet counterweight to the male obsession with discovery."
  - name: "M. Fridriksson"
    role: "A professor in Reykjavik; the party's local host"
    summary: "A learned Icelander who meets Professor Hardwigg in Reykjavik and explains Iceland's libraries, Literary Society and Mechanics' Institute. He tells the Professor of Saknussemm's fate and helps arrange a guide."
    analysis: "Fridriksson shows Iceland's culture of learning despite poverty, countering the Professor's expectations. He is also the link that leads the party to Hans and to Sneffels."
  - name: "Baron Trampe"
    role: "Governor of Iceland"
    summary: "The governor who receives the travellers formally at Reykjavik and helps with local arrangements before they set out for Sneffels."
    analysis: "Trampe's courtesy adds a sense of the real Iceland the travellers pass through, and his official welcome lends the expedition legitimacy."
