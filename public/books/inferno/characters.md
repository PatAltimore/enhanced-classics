---
title: "Inferno"
author: "Dante Alighieri"
year: 1320
book_slug: "inferno"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Dante (the Pilgrim)"
    role: "Narrator and protagonist; a Florentine poet in the middle of his life"
    summary: "Lost in a dark wood at the age of thirty-five, Dante is turned back by three beasts and then offered a way through Hell by Virgil. He descends through the nine circles, faints at the story of Francesca, weeps for the damned and sometimes hardens his heart. He crosses the body of Satan and emerges to see the stars."
    analysis: "The Pilgrim is Everyman and a particular exile, and the poem tracks his moral education as he learns to pity, then to judge, the sinners he meets. His reactions let readers feel both horror and sympathy, and his political anger at Florence runs through the whole journey."
  - name: "Virgil"
    role: "Roman poet; Dante's guide through Hell"
    summary: "The author of the Aeneid, whose shade is sent to lead Dante through Hell and Purgatory. He gets the travellers past Charon, Minos, Cerberus and the Minotaur, calms Dante's fears, and shelters him from the demons. He scolds Dante for staring at the sowers of discord."
    analysis: "Virgil stands for human reason and classical wisdom, which can guide Dante through sin but cannot enter Heaven. His protective tenderness and occasional stern rebukes make him a father figure, and his own place in Limbo shows the poem's limits on virtue without faith."
  - name: "Beatrice"
    role: "Dante's beloved; the heavenly source of his journey"
    summary: "The Florentine woman Dante loved in his youth, now in Heaven. She sends Virgil to rescue Dante when she sees him lost, with the help of the Virgin and Saint Lucia. She appears only in Virgil's account, in the second canto."
    analysis: "Beatrice is the origin of the whole rescue and the symbol of divine grace working through love. Though she never enters Hell, her name sets the poem's moral direction, and Virgil's reference to her explains why he agrees to go."
  - name: "Francesca da Rimini"
    role: "A lustful soul in the second circle"
    summary: "A noblewoman blown about by the storm of the lustful, who tells Dante how she and her brother-in-law Paolo read the story of Lancelot and fell in love. Her husband killed them both. Dante faints from pity."
    analysis: "Francesca's speech is the most famous in the Inferno, and it shows sin in the guise of romantic love. Her self-justifying words and her passive blame of the book test the reader's sympathy and Dante's own."
  - name: "Farinata degli Uberti"
    role: "Florentine Ghibelline leader; a heretic"
    summary: "A proud political leader who rises from his burning tomb as if he scorned Hell. He argues with Dante about Florentine factions, predicts Dante's exile, and says he defended Florence when everyone else wanted to destroy it."
    analysis: "Farinata is a model of stubborn greatness held captive by false belief. His scene joins personal pride, civic politics and heresy, and his prophecy ties Dante's journey to his own banishment."
  - name: "Brunetto Latini"
    role: "Dante's teacher; among the sodomites"
    summary: "A Florentine statesman and writer who taught Dante, and who now runs along the burning sand. He speaks with warmth and affection, foretells Dante's glory and recommends his own Treasure to him."
    analysis: "Brunetto is one of the poem's most poignant figures, a beloved mentor condemned by the poet who owes him so much. Dante's respect and his judgement coexist, which shows how complex the poem's idea of justice is."
  - name: "Pier delle Vigne"
    role: "Counsellor of Emperor Frederick II; a suicide"
    summary: "A chancellor accused of treason who killed himself in prison. He has become a thorny tree in the wood of the suicides, bleeding words when Dante breaks a twig. He swears he was loyal to his emperor and tells how souls are bound to their trees."
    analysis: "Pier's speech joins injured honour to despair, and the physical image of the wounded tree makes self-destruction vivid. His case also shows how courtly envy can ruin a faithful servant."
  - name: "Geryon"
    role: "The monster of fraud"
    summary: "A winged beast with a just man's face, a serpent's body and a scorpion's tail, who flies Dante and Virgil down the great cliff to Malebolge. Virgil negotiates the descent while Dante visits the usurers."
    analysis: "Geryon personifies fraud as something that looks honest but is dangerous underneath. His appearance marks the move from sins of violence to sins of deceit, the poem's central moral boundary."
  - name: "Vanni Fucci"
    role: "A thief of Pistoia; a blasphemer"
    summary: "A violent man who steals from a sacristy, then in Hell makes an obscene gesture at God. Snakes at once bind his arms and neck. He tells Dante of Florentine political troubles."
    analysis: "Vanni shows defiance in damnation, and his coarse insolence makes the thieves' ditch grotesque. His sudden punishment demonstrates that Hell is a place where God's justice is swift and fitting."
  - name: "Ulysses (Odysseus)"
    role: "Greek hero; a counsellor of fraud"
    summary: "Wrapped in a flame in the eighth circle with Diomedes, Ulysses tells how he refused to settle at home and sailed west past the Pillars of Hercules. He urged his old crew to seek knowledge beyond the world. A storm sank the ship in sight of Mount Purgatory."
    analysis: "Ulysses is both a hero and a warning, whose thirst for knowledge becomes a sin of overreaching. His speech is among the most eloquent in the poem and lets Dante weigh ambition against obedience to God."
  - name: "Guido da Montefeltro"
    role: "Ghibelline soldier and counsellor; a false adviser"
    summary: "A cunning Italian general who became a Franciscan and was persuaded by Pope Boniface VIII to give advice that would win a war by deceit. At his death a devil claims him, arguing that no one can be absolved for a sin he intends to commit."
    analysis: "Guido's case shows the corruption of the church, as the pope's absolution proves worthless. The devil's clever logic is a reminder that Hell takes legalistic fraud seriously."
  - name: "Bertran de Born"
    role: "Troubadour; a sower of discord"
    summary: "A poet and lord who set a king and his son against each other. He carries his severed head by the hair like a lantern, saying that because he divided those who should be united, his own head is divided from his body."
    analysis: "Bertran embodies the poem's idea of the punishment fitting the crime. His fate is a warning for a poet who uses his gifts to spread conflict."
  - name: "Count Ugolino"
    role: "Pisan nobleman; a traitor"
    summary: "A count who betrayed Pisa and was then locked in a tower to starve with his sons. In the ice he gnaws the skull of Archbishop Ruggieri, who imprisoned him, and tells how his children died. Dante does not say whether hunger or grief killed him."
    analysis: "Ugolino's tale is one of the most harrowing in the poem, joining traitor and victim in one figure. The ambiguity over whether he ate his sons invites readers to judge the limits of pity and horror."
  - name: "Satan (Dis)"
    role: "Ruler of Hell; trapped in the ice"
    summary: "A gigantic three-faced figure frozen to his waist at the center of Hell. He weeps and chews Judas, Brutus and Cassius in his three mouths, and beats his wings, which freeze the lake. Dante and Virgil climb down his body to leave Hell."
    analysis: "Satan is not a majestic ruler but an impotent, grotesque prisoner, the opposite of God's light and love. The poem makes the lowest point of Hell the place of cold treachery, and his body becomes the way out."
