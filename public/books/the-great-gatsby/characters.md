---
title: "The Great Gatsby"
author: "F. Scott Fitzgerald"
year: 1925
book_slug: "the-great-gatsby"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Nick Carraway"
    role: "Narrator; Daisy's cousin and Gatsby's neighbour"
    summary: "A Yale graduate and Great War veteran from the Midwest who moves to West Egg to learn the bond business. He rents the small house next to Gatsby's mansion, is drawn into the lives of his cousin Daisy, her husband Tom and the golfer Jordan Baker, and later arranges the reunion of Gatsby and Daisy. He tells the story a year or more afterwards, having returned home disillusioned."
    analysis: "Nick is both witness and moral measure. He claims to reserve all judgements, yet his account is shaped by his fascination with Gatsby and his eventual disgust at the Buchanans, so the reader must weigh how far to trust him. His Midwestern values frame East Coast glamour as something both seductive and corrupt, and his final verdict that Gatsby was worth the whole rotten crowd gives the novel its emotional centre."
  - name: "Jay Gatsby"
    role: "The title character; a self-made millionaire"
    summary: "Born James Gatz to poor farming parents in North Dakota, he reinvented himself as Jay Gatsby after meeting the copper magnate Dan Cody. He fell in love with Daisy Fay as a young officer in 1917, and builds his fortune, partly through bootlegging with Meyer Wolfsheim, and his mansion across the bay from her house so that she will return to him. He is shot in his pool by George Wilson, who wrongly believes Gatsby killed Myrtle."
    analysis: "Gatsby embodies the American Dream in both its promise and its illusion: he believes that wealth and will can repeat the past. His romantic hope is admirable and absurd at once, and his devotion to an idealised Daisy shows how longing can outrun reality. His lonely funeral exposes the hollowness of the society that enjoyed his parties."
  - name: "Daisy Buchanan"
    role: "Gatsby's lost love; Nick's cousin; Tom's wife"
    summary: "A beautiful, charming woman from Louisville who married the wealthy Tom Buchanan in 1919 while Gatsby was abroad. She is unhappy in her marriage, resumes a romance with Gatsby after five years, and then retreats to Tom when forced to choose. She is driving the car that kills Myrtle Wilson, but lets Gatsby take the blame."
    analysis: "Daisy is less a woman than the object of Gatsby's dream, and her voice, 'full of money,' ties desire to class. Her inability to leave Tom shows that her loyalty lies with security and status. Fitzgerald uses her to show how the careless rich shelter themselves from the consequences of their actions."
  - name: "Tom Buchanan"
    role: "Daisy's husband; a wealthy, aggressive aristocrat"
    summary: "A former Yale football star with inherited money, he lives in East Egg and keeps a mistress, Myrtle Wilson, in New York. He is physically powerful, bigoted and quick to bully. At the Plaza Hotel he exposes Gatsby's criminal connections to break his hold on Daisy, then lets George Wilson believe that Gatsby was Myrtle's driver and lover."
    analysis: "Tom represents the entrenched power of old money and its sense of entitlement. His hypocrisy, since he demands fidelity from Daisy while betraying her, and his racist talk of 'The Rise of the Colored Empires,' show how unearned privilege can be brutal. He is the antagonist who wins, and his survival with Daisy is part of the novel's bleak verdict."
  - name: "Jordan Baker"
    role: "Daisy's friend; a professional golfer; Nick's romantic interest"
    summary: "A poised, cynical young woman from Louisville who is known for a rumoured cheating scandal at a golf tournament. She tells Nick about Gatsby's past with Daisy and helps arrange their meeting. She and Nick drift into a brief relationship, which ends coldly after Gatsby's death."
    analysis: "Jordan stands for the modern, self-sufficient woman of the 1920s and also for the era's carelessness and dishonesty. Nick's remark that she is 'incurably dishonest' tests his own claim to tolerance, and their breakup marks his turn away from the East."
  - name: "Myrtle Wilson"
    role: "Tom's mistress; George Wilson's wife"
    summary: "A vital, restless woman who runs a garage with her husband in the Valley of Ashes. She meets Tom on a train, takes an apartment in New York paid for by him, and plays at being wealthy. After a quarrel with George she runs to a yellow car she believes is Tom's and is killed by it."
    analysis: "Myrtle shows the cost of aspiring to a higher class that will not admit you. Her vitality contrasts with Daisy's languor, and Tom's casual violence against her, breaking her nose, anticipates his larger cruelty. Her death is the novel's turning point, and she is another casualty of the Buchanans' recklessness."
  - name: "George Wilson"
    role: "Myrtle's husband; a struggling garage owner"
    summary: "A worn-down mechanic in the Valley of Ashes who is devoted to his wife and ignorant of her affair until shortly before her death. After she is killed he tracks the car to Gatsby, shoots him in his pool and then kills himself."
    analysis: "George is the poor man destroyed by the rich, and his grief is manipulated by Tom into vengeance on the wrong target. His belief that the eyes of Doctor T. J. Eckleburg are the eyes of God ties his tragedy to the novel's theme of moral emptiness."
  - name: "Meyer Wolfsheim"
    role: "Gatsby's criminal associate"
    summary: "A gambler with a taste for cufflinks made of human molars, who Gatsby says fixed the 1919 World Series. He gave Gatsby his start in business and links him to bootlegging, but refuses to attend the funeral."
    analysis: "Wolfsheim reveals the illegal origins of Gatsby's fortune and the corruption at the root of the Jazz Age boom. His refusal to show gratitude or loyalty underlines how isolated Gatsby is at the end."
  - name: "Dan Cody"
    role: "Gatsby's early mentor"
    summary: "A rich copper-mining magnate who took the teenage Gatsby aboard his yacht and taught him about wealth and polish. He died leaving Gatsby money, but his mistress Ella Kaye prevented Gatsby from receiving the legacy."
    analysis: "Cody is the model for Gatsby's reinvention and shows the source of his ideal of the self-made man. His story also anticipates the way wealth passes to those with the right connections rather than those who deserve it."
  - name: "Henry C. Gatz"
    role: "Gatsby's father"
    summary: "A farmer from Minnesota who arrives for the funeral, proud of his son and carrying a copy of Hopalong Cassidy that Gatsby annotated as a boy with a self-improvement schedule."
    analysis: "Gatz proves the truth of Gatsby's origins and shows the boyhood ambition behind the myth. He is one of the few mourners at the funeral, which makes the loneliness of Gatsby's death all the more affecting."
  - name: "Owl Eyes"
    role: "A guest at Gatsby's parties"
    summary: "A bespectacled drunk who discovers that the books in Gatsby's library are real, though uncut. He is one of the few guests who attends the funeral."
    analysis: "Owl Eyes sees through the surface of Gatsby's world and admires the thoroughness of the illusion. His appearance at the funeral, with 'Why, my God! They used to go there by the hundreds,' voices the novel's judgement on the party crowd."
