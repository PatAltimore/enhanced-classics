---
title: "The Tale of Genji"
author: "Murasaki Shikibu"
year: 1008
book_slug: "the-tale-of-genji"
license: "public-domain"

characters:
  - name: "Genji (Hikaru Genji)"
    role: "Hero; the Emperor's son, made a commoner of the Minamoto clan"
    summary: "The Emperor's son by the Kiritsubo lady, who loses his mother at three and is made a commoner for his safety. Admired for his beauty and talents, he moves through a series of love affairs, marries Aoi without love, and secretly fathers a child with his stepmother Fujitsubo. He brings the girl Murasaki home to raise and, after Aoi's death, makes her his wife."
    analysis: "Genji is both an ideal and a flawed figure: charming, kind and generous, yet careless of the women who love him. Murasaki lets his adventures bring pain to others, so that the book keeps asking what the cost of his charm is. His longing for a lost mother, which draws him to Fujitsubo and Murasaki, drives the plot."
  - name: "Fujitsubo"
    role: "The Emperor's consort, later Empress; Genji's secret love"
    summary: "A princess brought to court because she resembles the dead Kiritsubo lady, so Genji grows up thinking of her almost as a mother. Her first meeting with Genji as a young man leaves her pregnant, and she raises the child, officially the Emperor's son, in torment. The Emperor then makes her Empress to protect the boy."
    analysis: "Fujitsubo is the origin of Genji's deepest longing, and her guilt and restraint contrast with his impulsiveness. As the keeper of the book's central secret she makes every later scene of court ritual and public praise double-edged. Murasaki's resemblance to her explains Genji's attachment to the younger girl."
  - name: "Murasaki"
    role: "Fujitsubo's niece; the girl Genji raises and later marries"
    summary: "A child of about ten when Genji first glimpses her in the hills, she is the granddaughter of a nun and the daughter of Prince Hyōbukyō. After her grandmother's death Genji takes her to his palace, teaches her music and writing, and after Aoi's death marries her. She grows from a frightened child into his closest companion."
    analysis: "Murasaki is both Genji's chosen heir to Fujitsubo and a character in her own right, with a quick mind and strong feelings. The relationship can be read as tender or as exploitative, and the novel does not settle the question. Her name also became the author's own nickname."
  - name: "Aoi"
    role: "Genji's first wife; the Minister of the Left's daughter"
    summary: "A proud, dignified woman four years older than Genji, who was married to him as a boy. She meets his infidelities with cold silence, becomes pregnant late in the story, and is humiliated by her servants' part in the carriage quarrel. A spirit afflicts her, she bears a son, and she dies suddenly."
    analysis: "Aoi is the stern, correct wife against whom Genji's other attachments are measured, and her death forces him to see what he failed to value. Her father's grief and the closing visit at New Year show how deeply the family loved her. She also gives the chapter its title."
  - name: "Lady Rokujō"
    role: "Genji's older mistress; widow of a crown prince"
    summary: "A refined, proud widow, named after her mansion on Sixth Avenue, who has been Genji's lover for years and is hurt by his neglect. At the Kamo festival Aoi's servants push her carriage aside in front of the crowd. Afterwards a spirit speaks through Aoi in her voice, and she smells exorcists' incense in her own hair."
    analysis: "Rokujō shows how pride and love can turn destructive, and how Heian belief in possession could voice a woman's repressed rage. The book never says she meant to harm Aoi, which makes her one of its most sympathetic and disturbing figures. Genji's recognition of her voice ends their affair."
  - name: "Tō no Chūjō"
    role: "Genji's friend and rival; Aoi's brother"
    summary: "The son of the Minister of the Left and the Emperor's sister, and Genji's closest friend at court. He leads the rainy-night discussion of women, shares Genji's dances, and teases him by following him in disguise. He is also Yūgao's former lover, though he does not learn of her fate."
    analysis: "Tō no Chūjō is Genji's foil: equally well-born and charming but more open and less guarded. Their friendly rivalry gives the book its comic scenes and shows that Genji's life is shared with equals. He also links Yūgao's story to the main plot."
  - name: "The Emperor (Kiritsubo Emperor)"
    role: "Genji's father; the reigning Emperor"
    summary: "A sovereign whose devotion to the low-ranking Kiritsubo lady causes scandal, and who grieves for her long after her death. He turns to Fujitsubo because of her likeness to the dead lady, and he dotes on her son, never guessing the child is Genji's. Later he abdicates, and remains an influential retired Emperor."
    analysis: "The Emperor's love defines the world Genji is born into and his protective affection explains Genji's privileges. His innocence about the child is the cruellest irony of the book. He also stands for the court's old, gentle order before the political tensions that follow."
  - name: "The Kiritsubo Lady"
    role: "Genji's mother; a lady of modest rank"
    summary: "Favoured far above her station by the Emperor, she is persecuted by jealous rivals and withers under the strain. She bears Genji, falls ill and is carried home to die while he is only three. Her grieving mother and the Emperor's longing for her fill the first chapter."
    analysis: "Her fate sets the pattern of beauty destroyed by court envy, and her death is the loss that shapes Genji. Everyone he later loves, from Fujitsubo to Murasaki, is measured against her memory. She is also named for the chapter of her apartment, as many characters are."
  - name: "Princess Kōkiden"
    role: "Genji's chief enemy at court; mother of the Heir Apparent"
    summary: "The Emperor's senior consort, the daughter of the Minister of the Right and mother of the Heir Apparent. She resents the Kiritsubo lady and Genji, sneers at his dancing, and bitterly opposes Fujitsubo's elevation. Her family, the Minister of the Right and her sister Oborozukiyo, form the rival faction."
    analysis: "Kōkiden represents the political opposition to Genji and the Emperor's affection for him. Her animosity gives the court its factional tension and prepares for the later struggle. She also gives Genji the opportunity for the encounter with her sister that starts the next phase."
  - name: "Utsusemi"
    role: "The young wife of Iyo no Suke; a lady who refuses Genji"
    summary: "A woman of middling rank, married to an elderly provincial governor, whom Genji enters at night after the rainy-night talk. She slips away, leaving her robe, and refuses to meet him again despite her feelings. She leaves for the provinces with her husband, to whom Genji returns her scarf."
    analysis: "Utsusemi is the one woman who resists Genji, which makes her more interesting to him. Her restraint, her self-awareness and her cicada-shell poems make her a model of dignity within narrow limits. She also tests the chapter's argument about the worth of the middle-ranking woman."
  - name: "Yūgao"
    role: "A mysterious lady of a humble house; Tō no Chūjō's lost lover"
    summary: "A gentle, timid woman whom Genji finds through the white flowers on her fence and visits in disguise. He takes her to a deserted mansion where she dies suddenly in the night after a spirit appears in his dream. We later learn she was the mother of Tō no Chūjō's daughter."
    analysis: "Yūgao represents the charm of the unpretentious and the fragility of love, and her death is the novel's first major tragedy. Her sudden vanishing shows how much of Genji's world is built on concealment. Her daughter becomes important in later chapters."
  - name: "Koremitsu"
    role: "Genji's loyal retainer; his foster-brother"
    summary: "The son of Genji's old nurse, who acts as his go-between and companion on secret expeditions. He finds out about Yūgao, escorts Genji in disguise, and handles the discreet removal of her body and her funeral. He later helps Genji carry off Murasaki and orders the ritual cakes."
    analysis: "Koremitsu is Genji's confidant and the practical hand behind his romances, with a servant's tact and his own love affairs. His presence shows how much a nobleman's secrets depend on retainers. He is also a quiet comic figure, who plays the shrewd man of affairs."
  - name: "The Saffron-Flower Princess (Suetsumuhana)"
    role: "The daughter of Prince Hitachi; a shy, impoverished princess"
    summary: "A lonely, music-loving princess living in a decaying mansion, to whom Genji is drawn by tales of her talent. When he finally sees her, he finds her extremely plain, with a long nose red at the tip, yet his pity leads him to support her. She sends him a clumsy gift of clothes and poems at New Year."
    analysis: "She tests whether Genji's kindness is real once beauty is absent, and the comedy is also compassionate. Murasaki asks the reader to judge his cruel jokes and his generosity together. Her unworldly loyalty is a counterpoint to the court's fashionable cynicism."
  - name: "Oborozukiyo"
    role: "Kōkiden's younger sister; Genji's lady of the hazy moon"
    summary: "A young woman whom Genji meets in the dark at Kōkiden's palace after the flower banquet, when he hears her humming a poem about the hazy moon. They exchange fans but not names, and he later finds her by her answering verse among the ladies at the wisteria feast. She is destined for the Heir Apparent."
    analysis: "Oborozukiyo shows Genji's recklessness and the dangerous mix of passion and politics, since she belongs to his enemies' family. Her witty replies make her a match for him, and her name from the poem shows how a moment can name a person. The affair prepares a later crisis."
